🥤 Smoothie Prep Tips
1
Pull the cup from the freezer the moment you wake up
Everything frozen together = one solid puck on the blade. Set it on the counter first thing and let it sit while you shower and get ready — 5–10 min is all it needs.
2
Don't fill the cup past halfway when prepping it the night before
You're adding milk in the morning on top of frozen ingredients. If the cup is already full of frozen stuff, milk has nowhere to go and it overflows when blending.
3
Add milk slowly — stop before the MAX line
Pour milk to just below the MAX fill line. Frozen ingredients + liquid expand into a vortex — going over the line is what causes the overflow.
4
Swap whole almonds for almond butter only
Whole almonds bounce off the blade and never fully break down. Almond butter blends smoothly and gives you the same nutrition — add a spoonful to the cup when prepping.
5
Blend 45 seconds straight
One continuous run beats pulsing every time. Let it go the full 45 sec without stopping.

🟢 Lemon herb salmon + roasted asparagus
~45g protein~500 calReheats: excellent🟢 Low mess🧹 Cleanup: ~5 min
Salmon = omega-3 + nerve health · Asparagus = anti-inflammatory
📋 Directions (~20 min)
  • Dishes: 1 sheet pan, paper towels, knife, cutting board (lemon only)
  • Preheat oven to 400°F. Lightly oil the sheet pan.
  • Toss asparagus in olive oil, salt, and pepper. Spread on sheet pan.
  • Minute 0 — asparagus goes in.
  • Pat salmon bone dry with paper towels. Season with salt, pepper, and herbs (dill or parsley). Place skin-side down on same pan.
  • Minute 3 — salmon goes in. Skin-side down, do not flip.
  • Minute 8 — flip asparagus for even browning. Leave salmon alone.
  • Minute 15 — both come out. Salmon done when it flakes easily and center is just opaque.
  • Squeeze lemon over both immediately. Serve.
  • Cleanup: Rinse 1 sheet pan, toss paper towels. Done in ~5 min.
💡 Tips for extra crispy
Asparagus

The Psychological Illusions
"The Illusion of Enjoyment"
Most of what you think you enjoy about drinking isn't pleasure arriving — it's anxiety leaving. The "ahh" after the first sip is your brain getting relief from low-level withdrawal it was already running from the last session. You're not enjoying the drink. You're ending a discomfort you didn't even know you were carrying.
"Alcohol Is Not a Stimulant"
It feels like a stimulant early — talkative, energized, confident. That's your brain fighting the depressant effect, over-compensating to restore balance. The stimulant feeling is your brain in emergency mode. The depressant always wins eventually, which is why every big night ends in a crash, flatness, or regret.
"The Top-Up Trap"
The 1st drink creates a small chemical dip as your brain counters it with glutamate. The 2nd drink feels necessary because you're topping up the hole the 1st one dug. You're not drinking to feel good — you're drinking to feel normal again. The urge for the 2nd drink is manufactured by the first one. You know the trick now.
"The Decision Is Never at the Bar"
By the time someone hands you a drink at midnight, your prefrontal cortex — the part that thinks about Barry's at 8am — is already partially offline. Porter's key insight: the decision happens hours earlier. Saturday afternoon, sober, full-brain. That's when you set the limit. Not at the bar with a compromised brain.
"Borrowed Confidence"
The courage you feel talking to women when drinking isn't real confidence — it's your prefrontal cortex going offline so you stop registering social risk. You're not more capable. You're less able to feel the fear. Cincinnati proved the real version exists — you were sober, you felt the discomfort, you acted anyway. That built something. Drinking builds nothing.
"Everyone Is Managing Anxiety"
Porter makes this point directly: most people at the bar are managing their own withdrawal and anxiety, collectively convincing each other they're having a great time. Nobody wants to admit the environment is just okay and they're mainly there to feel normal. The group pressure you feel to drink isn't social joy — it's a shared coping mechanism.
